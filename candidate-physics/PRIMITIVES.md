# Local primitives and invariants

## Site

A site is a location in a locally connected three-dimensional space. It holds
limited state and interacts with itself, adjacent sites, the local medium, and
present boundary values. Its state is entirely local.

## Medium

The medium supplies finite local capacity and permits transfer between adjacent
sites. Candidate properties include occupancy, available capacity, local
pressure, resistance, permeability, and agitation.

These properties arise from local state and bounded host conditions.

## Mobile unit

A mobile unit carries a local directional state and may move to an adjacent
site when local capacity permits. A unit may pass, scatter, reflect, join a
configuration, separate, transform, or return its occupied capacity.

Movement follows present local conditions.

## Bond

A bond is a local relation among compatible units. Formation depends on local
geometry and present conditions. A newly formed bond may mature through
continued compatibility.

A mature bond's duration emerges from local conditions. Later encounters may
preserve, alter, fragment, release, or terminate the configuration.

## Connected configuration

A connected configuration is defined by relations among its parts rather than
by one fixed embedding. It may deform while preserving selected adjacency,
order, closure, or internal flow.

Passage through a narrow region may alter its geometry without necessarily
destroying its relational continuity.

## Passage

A passage is an emergent region whose local permeability differs from its
surroundings.

The narrowest stable passage may admit only one connected configuration across
its width. Its serial order changes only through local transformation. Global
parallelism may arise from many branching passages operating concurrently.

## Strand

A strand is an ordered connected configuration. Its geometry may change nearby
transfer, alignment, joining, separation, or transformation.

A strand becomes template-capable only if local complementarity can assemble a
related strand beside it and permit later separation. Complementarity must be a
local geometric relation.

Copying may be imperfect. Variation must arise from local competition,
interruption, resource conditions, or physical entropy rather than a global
mutation command.

## Resource coupling

Bounded host conditions may change cadence, local capacity, resistance,
agitation, or transformation thresholds through direct physical possibility.

## Transformation and decay

Local configurations may transform or release occupied capacity. Decay must be
a possible local consequence of geometry, saturation, incompatibility, or
resource conditions.

Local conditions determine release and removal.

## Invariants

1. Every interaction is local.
2. State contains local physical relations.
3. Structure begins without positional, identifier, or naming privilege.
4. Continued existence depends on local conditions.
5. Copying, if possible, is implemented through local complementarity.
6. Narrow passages preserve serial order unless a local transformation changes
   the connected configuration.
7. Runtime interpretation, counters, and semantic logs are absent.
8. Host coupling changes physical conditions, not declared goals.
9. This directory remains separate from the current runtime.

## Mechanical questions for any executable candidate

- Can mobile units traverse open space without immediate loss or explosion?
- Can compatible local encounters form configurations with conditional
  persistence?
- Can a connected configuration deform through a one-configuration-wide
  passage and retain relational continuity?
- Can passage ordering persist across branching and delay?
- Can a strand alter nearby local interactions without an interpreter?
- Can local complementarity assemble and separate a related strand?
- Can changed transfer conditions help recreate a configuration that helped
  create those conditions?
- Can resource scarcity alter possibilities without functioning as a reward?
- Can decay return capacity without requiring a global decision?
