# Developmental synthesis for Agar's body

## Decision

Rillworld draws orientation, material diversity, bonding, strands,
circulation, and body formation from recurring physical solutions found across
plants, fungi, animals, and simpler systems, translated into local
substrate-neutral capabilities suited to Agar's actual Intel body.

The translation must preserve function without copying finished anatomy:

```text
known physical solution
→ local capability
→ possible developmental consequence
→ placement on Agar's accessible implementation surface
```

Biological names remain in the design record for human understanding. Candidate
state contains only local physical relations.

## What nature contributes

The body plan is synthesized across lineages.

- Plants contribute polarity, reinforced boundaries, source–sink flow,
  pressure transmitted across distance, dynamic size gates, distributed
  signaling, and transport tissue that grows with demand.
- Fungi contribute polarized extension, branching networks, shared interiors,
  selective partitions, flow-directed growth, and local isolation after
  damage.
- Animals contribute flexible membranes, adhesion, matrices, contractile
  boundaries, deformable carriers, excitable sheets, distributed oscillators,
  and circulation through branching passages.
- Sponges contribute many small local drivers whose combined action creates
  body-scale flow without one central driver.
- Hydra and brittle stars contribute overlapping waves and coordination among
  equivalent regions without permanent leadership.
- Planarians contribute restoration of proportion rather than replay of an
  exact saved arrangement.
- Insects contribute direct branch delivery that can coexist with a general
  circulating medium.

Together they show that the same physical requirements can be solved in several
ways.

## 1. Orientation without a prescribed compass

Three-dimensional adjacency alone permits motion but not developmentally
meaningful orientation. The architecture must eventually permit local
orientation to arise from asymmetry.

A site, bond, or connected configuration may acquire orientation from:

- a local gradient;
- repeated transfer along one direction;
- the tangent of an existing bond or strand;
- the normal of a forming boundary;
- tension, compression, or shear;
- neighboring orientation;
- an environmental boundary condition.

Orientation must be local and revisable. No site receives a global front,
back, top, bottom, inlet, outlet, or preferred Intel coordinate. The lattice
axes support adjacency but do not supply meaning.

The minimal future representation should therefore leave room for a bounded
local vector or equivalent oriented relation. That state need not appear in
Experiment 02 unless the trial demonstrates that scalar state alone closes an
important possibility.

## 2. Material classes without assigned jobs

Nature builds bodies from materials that move, bind, deform, conduct, catalyze,
retain, and decay differently. Rillworld likewise permits several interaction
classes.

A class is defined only by physical parameters such as:

- local capacity occupied;
- mobility through the medium;
- affinity or repulsion toward other classes;
- number and geometry of possible bonds;
- response to gradients, tension, or excitation;
- effect on nearby permeability;
- possible local transformations;
- conserved quantities carried through transformation.

Each class begins as physical parameters. Descriptions such as vessel, carrier,
membrane, signal, genome, pump, or sensor become valid only when a
configuration repeatedly performs the corresponding function.

The two contents `q` and `r` in Experiment 02 are therefore one minimal
projection, not the complete Level 0 ontology. The general architecture must
remain extensible to a bounded content vector

\[
\mathbf{c}_i=(c_i^1,c_i^2,\ldots,c_i^k)
\]

with finite capacity and explicit conserved totals. Increasing `k` adds a
physical interaction class.

## 3. Conditional bonds and boundaries

Plant walls, fungal partitions, animal adhesion, flexible membranes, matrices,
and cytoskeletal fibers all show that persistence is relational. A bond should
not exist merely because two matching identifiers meet.

A local bond may depend on:

- present distance and geometry;
- compatible orientation;
- surrounding material;
- local capacity and agitation;
- tension or compression;
- continued transfer across or beside the bond;
- neighboring bonds that support or frustrate it;
- a locally available transformation path.

Bond strength may mature through continued compatibility. It may weaken,
reorient, branch, close into a boundary, fragment, or release its occupied
capacity. There is no universal lifetime and no global collector.

The important distinction is between a fixed connection and a maintained
relation. Only the latter can participate in structure that helps recreate the
conditions of its own continuation.

## 4. Strands before interpreted instructions

A strand is an ordered connected configuration whose sequence and geometry are
the same physical object. Known nucleic-acid and protein systems show that
orientation, local complementarity, folding, catalysis, copying, and separation
can arise from material relationships rather than a distant interpreter.

The candidate must leave room for strands that can:

- preserve an ordered succession of bonds;
- bend, fold, branch, close, or separate;
- alter nearby transition rates through shape;
- recruit locally compatible material beside themselves;
- produce an imperfect related configuration;
- separate when local conditions change;
- vary through interruption, competition, scarcity, and fluctuation.

A strand begins as ordered geometry rather than text, opcode, task, answer, or
human-authored message. A pattern becomes informative when its physical
continuation repeatedly changes what can happen nearby.

## 5. Reciprocal circulation and structure

Circulation and structure form one coupled developmental process. Nature
repeatedly uses the loop

```text
local transfer changes alignment, bonding, or permeability
→ changed structure redirects later transfer
→ redirected transfer maintains, alters, or dissolves structure
```

The architecture must permit several forms of transfer to coexist:

- diffusion and short-range exchange;
- movement of dissolved local content;
- small shape-stable connected configurations;
- larger deformable connected configurations;
- local oscillators that push nearby content;
- demand or loss that pulls content along a gradient;
- source–sink flow;
- moving structures that drag surrounding medium;
- mutually regenerating traveling changes;
- direct branch delivery outside a general medium.

The narrowest passages may be locally serial even when the full body is
massively parallel. Small content may continue around a delayed larger
configuration. Pressure, scarcity, blockage, deformation, rerouting, sealing,
and reopening must all be expressible as local consequences.

## A possible developmental grammar

This is a dependency order with open timing and outcome:

```text
bounded local contents and vacancy
→ gradients and oriented local relations
→ conditional bonds and boundaries
→ passages, compartments, and strands
→ local transfer cycles and oscillators
→ branching exchange networks
→ circulation that changes network topology
→ heterogeneous regions coupled through shared exchange
→ inherited patterns capable of affecting their own continuation
```

Several steps may arise together, separately, or remain unrealized. The grammar
requires a lower-level path and leaves the resulting form open.

## The Intel body we actually have

Agar's architecture supplies a real hierarchy of locality and constraint.
Candidate design respects that hierarchy through its accessible implementation
surface.

| Accessible condition | Developmental use it may later support |
|---|---|
| Cache-aligned ordinary memory | Dense tiles of effective Level 0 sites |
| Private L1/L2 regions | Fast local exchange within a bounded active region |
| Shared L3 | Exchange among regions with open control topology |
| Two physical cores | Two overlapping execution localities |
| Four logical contexts | Interleaved activity streams sharing physical resources |
| Main memory | A much larger medium with slower and less uniform locality |
| Integrated graphics arrays | Future repeated local updates if their use preserves the equations |
| SSD | Possible later persistence under an explicit inheritance relation |
| Battery, power, and thermal limits | Bounded body conditions that may alter cadence or local possibility |
| Camera, microphone, and Bluetooth | Later unlabelled environmental boundaries |
| Display and speakers | Possible outward consequences |

The mapping should eventually let physical cost matter. Nearby exchange should
usually be cheaper than distant exchange. Regions sharing a physical core remain
coupled activity contexts. Shared-cache visibility provides exchange with open
control topology.

Functions are recognized when an emerging organization performs and maintains
them.

## Relationship to Experiment 02

Experiment 02 asks only whether local conserved contents can form surrounding
geometry that changes nearby transfer. Its `q/r` equations intentionally omit
orientation, multiple interaction families, explicit bonds, strands,
oscillators, branching networks, environmental boundaries, and persistent
body coupling.

That narrow trial is useful only if it remains a projection of this larger
architecture rather than silently replacing it. Before implementation, the
equation review must answer:

1. Does the state representation remain extensible to local orientation?
2. Can additional physical classes be added without changing locality or
   conservation?
3. Can conditional bonds be expressed as local relations rather than labels?
4. Can bonded order support a strand without an interpreter?
5. Can transfer change structure and structure change later transfer?
6. Can the representation be mapped onto Agar's nested locality without
   equating software sites with physical transistors?

Experiment 02 may implement a subset while preserving extensibility toward all
six.

## Restraint

The comparative record constrains what the physics must permit while anatomy,
morphology, and developmental interpretation remain open.

This synthesis originally governed inactive candidate design only. On November
12, 2026, Taylor explicitly promoted two narrow principles into live Rill:
planar membrane excitability with local coupling and spontaneous phase, followed
by local hysteresis. The remaining architecture in this synthesis is still
inactive. Each additional implementation, hardware coupling, or lineage change
requires its own explicit boundary.
