# Comparative plant, fungal, and simple-animal architectures

## Method

This pass compares living architectures along the same functional dimensions:

1. transport medium;
2. driving mechanism;
3. smallest passage and gating;
4. sensing and integration;
5. repair and remodeling;
6. growth and proportionality;
7. characteristic failure;
8. design question contributed to Rillworld.

The goal is to identify physical possibilities that remain effective across
distributed bodies, including bodies with several pumps, diffuse integration,
or continually remodeling networks.

## Plants: transport without one pump

### Xylem

Water extraction at leaves lowers local pressure. Cohesion and pressure
gradients transmit that effect through xylem toward roots and soil. The
transport system therefore can be pulled from a consuming boundary rather than
pushed by a central actuator.

Mature xylem conduits are reinforced structures that can conduct after their
cells have completed development. After cavitation or blockage, the growing
body may produce additional pathways around damaged conduits.

Rillworld consequence: environmental loss, transformation, or demand at one
boundary might create a gradient that reorganizes distant circulation.

### Phloem and cambium

Phloem moves photoassimilates and signals between sources and sinks. Separate
transport files may carry material in different directions at the same time.
The vascular cambium continually produces new xylem and phloem as the body
grows, and environmental conditions alter vessel production and dimensions.

Rillworld consequence: flow direction need not be permanent. Local production
and consumption can establish pressure, while circulation participates in
growing the network it will later use.

### Plasmodesmata

Plasmodesmata are small intercellular channels with dynamic size-exclusion
limits. Their geometry and permeability can change with tissue type,
development, light, temperature, infection, and callose deposition.

Rillworld consequence: the smallest passage should be capable of selective
gating. Small dissolved states may pass while a larger carrier waits, deforms,
or is excluded. Passage walls may close, widen, or reopen locally.

### Cytoplasmic streaming

In large plant cells, myosin motors carry organelles along polarized actin
tracks. The moving cargo drags the surrounding cytoplasm, generating coherent
streaming that distributes other material.

Rillworld consequence: transported structures may help generate the flow that
carries additional structures. Pump and cargo can remain fluid roles.

### Systemic waves

Plants propagate interacting electrical, hydraulic, calcium, and
reactive-oxygen waves. Calcium can activate reactive-oxygen production, which
can activate further calcium entry in neighboring cells. Local injury can
therefore create a self-propagating systemic response.

Rillworld consequence: integration may occur through mutually regenerating
waves rather than a central processor receiving every signal.

## Fungi: a network whose flows alter its growth

Fungal mycelia combine diffusion, cytoplasmic streaming, vesicle transport,
growth-induced mass flow, and evaporative movement. Long-distance resource
translocation supports growing tips in patchy environments. Network structure
controls resource movement, while resource movement changes where and how the
network grows.

Porous septa preserve continuity between compartments under ordinary
conditions. When a region is damaged, pores may be plugged to isolate that
region while the larger network continues.

Rillworld consequences:

- circulation may directly reshape future topology;
- the larger network may continue around isolated local damage;
- selective isolation and later reopening are possible;
- several transport mechanisms may coexist in one connected medium;
- inherited patterns may be distributed across a shared continuous interior.

## Simple animals: coordination before centralization

### Sponges

Sponges move environmental water through a branching aquiferous system.
Thousands of choanocytes beat flagella inside many chambers, collectively
overcoming canal resistance. Water enters through small ostia, crosses chambers
and canals, and exits through one or more oscula.

Sponges lack conventional neurons and muscles, yet they modulate flow, narrow
canals, halt feeding currents, perform whole-body contractions or deflations,
and flush debris. Neighbor-to-neighbor chemical communication and, in some
lineages, syncytial electrical conduction coordinate these responses.

Rillworld consequence: many tiny local pumps can create body-scale circulation
and protective flushing through distributed coordination.

### Hydra

Hydra has diffuse ectodermal and endodermal nerve nets. Distinct distributed
subnetworks produce different electrical rhythms.
Epithelial muscle layers also propagate excitation through gap junctions, and
body mechanics transform those waves into contraction, elongation, bending,
and locomotion.

Rillworld consequence: integration may arise from several overlapping waves
and excitable sheets. A body may coordinate globally even when no location
receives every input.

### Planarians

Planarians lack a conventional circulatory system. A highly branched
gastrovascular tract distributes metabolites throughout the body, while tissue
diffusion and parenchymal cells contribute to exchange and storage.

Distributed pluripotent neoblasts support continuous cell turnover, growth,
shrinkage, and regeneration. After amputation, remaining gut branches remodel
to restore both function and global proportion.

Rillworld consequence: repair may proceed through whole-network reproportioning.

### Brittle stars

Brittle stars coordinate five similar arms through radial nerve cords connected
by a circumoral ring. Coordination depends on radial topology and changing
relationships among arms.

Rillworld consequence: repeated equivalent regions may negotiate temporary
direction and coordination without assigning one region permanent leadership.

### Insects

Insects largely separate respiratory delivery from general fluid circulation.
Air enters through gated spiracles and travels through branching tracheae and
tracheoles directly toward tissues. Terminal tracheoles may be only 0.1–1 µm in
diameter. The network grows and remodels with body size and oxygen demand.

Rillworld consequence: selected resources may use direct branch delivery while
other materials remain in the general medium.

## Combined requirements contributed to the candidate

The comparative pass adds these required possibilities:

1. Flow may be pushed, pulled, source–sink driven, motor generated, or
   self-propagating.
2. Several transport modes may coexist in one body.
3. Small states may bypass large carriers through separate passages.
4. Passages may gate by size and local condition.
5. Walls and passages may grow, narrow, seal, branch, and reopen.
6. Distributed local pumps may generate whole-network flow.
7. Moving cargo may help generate its surrounding flow.
8. Flow may alter the future topology of the network.
9. Damage may be isolated without stopping the whole system.
10. Repair may restore global proportion rather than replay a saved local form.
11. Several overlapping waves may integrate the body without one center.
12. Repeated equivalent regions may coordinate without permanent leadership.
13. Direct resource delivery may coexist with a general circulating medium.

Experiment 02 begins after its design states which of these possibilities it
preserves and which it deliberately excludes.
